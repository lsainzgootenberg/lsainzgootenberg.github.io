Photography

10805

August 2024, Portra 400

"The past is like the scene of a crime: if the deed itself is irrecoverable, its traces may still remain. From these traces, markers that point towards a past presence, to something that has happened in this place, a (re)construction, if not a simulacrum, of the event can be pieced together."

– Annette Kuhn, Family Secrets

Color photography in the mid 20th-century created the mass-market point-and-shoot family portrait; I conceived of ‘10805’ as another kind of family archive. This is my attempt to make legible a “living image of a dead thing”, in the words of Roland Barthes. The feeling of a photograph that holds the essence of a loved one is wholly subjective, so I attempted to create a portrait of my grandma through her home after her death.

The goal was to make these feelings legible to others through the absence of a person. I hoped these images would unsettle the stillness of the home in its final moments.

In my last few days in the house before it was sold, I followed the rhythm and the movement of the space. The rocking chair in motion through the window, the handmade photo collages, her glamour, knickknacks, lipstick tubes. Shoes still positioned by the foot of the bed, ready to be worn. Lights still on and laundry left hanging.
